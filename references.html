    References

    Foundational Papers

    Constant Potential Method

    1. Siepmann, J. I.; Sprik, M. (1995). “Influence of electrostatic interactions on the simulated growth of copper clusters on the copper (100) surface.” J. Chem. Phys. 102, 511. DOI: 10.1063/1.469434

      The original formulation of constant potential MD. Introduced the idea of constraining electrode potentials during MD simulation.

    2. Reed, S. K.; Lanning, O. J.; Madden, P. A. (2007). “Electrochemical interface between an ionic liquid and a model metallic electrode.” J. Chem. Phys. 126, 084704. DOI: 10.1063/1.2434111

      Modern reformulation enabling practical MD simulations with constant potential. Introduced the capacitance matrix formalism.

    3. Gingrich, T. L. (2010). “Gaussian charge distributions for electrostatic boundary conditions in molecular dynamics simulations.” MSc thesis, University of Michigan.

      Systematic treatment of Gaussian smearing for constant potential method. Gives practical guidance on eta parameter selection.

    ELECTRODE Package

    1. Ahrens-Iwers, B. S. R.; Meissner, R. H. (2021). “The ELECTRODE package for LAMMPS: A user’s guide.” J. Chem. Phys. 155, 104104. DOI: 10.1063/5.0064855

      The primary reference for the LAMMPS ELECTRODE package. Contains installation instructions, syntax details, and validation.

    2. Ahrens-Iwers, B. S. R.; Tee, S.; Meissner, R. H. (2022). “Electrode charges as variables in constant-potential molecular dynamics simulations.” J. Chem. Phys. 157, 084801. DOI: 10.1063/5.0098793

      Detailed validation of the ELECTRODE package. Shows accuracy of computed capacitance and charge distributions.

    Review Articles

    1. Pastewka, L. (2023). “Constant electrode charge vs. constant electrode potential: A tutorial review.” Curr. Opin. Electrochem. 38, 101234.

      Accessible introduction to the difference between constant charge and constant potential methods. Good for understanding when each is appropriate.

    2. Salanne, M.; Rotenberg, B.; Jinnouchi, R. (2022). “Classical DFT for electrochemical systems: A practical guide to primitive model electrolytes.” In Computational Electrochemistry (eds. M. S. and B. R.). Springer.

      Connection between DFT and classical MD for electrode interfaces.

    Force Fields and Water Models

    1. Joung, I. S.; Cheatham, T. E. (2008). “Determination of alkali and halide monovalent ion parameters for use in explicitly solvated biomolecular simulations.” J. Phys. Chem. B 112, 9020. DOI: 10.1021/jp8001614

      Ion parameters for SPC/E water: Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, etc. The standard reference for ion LJ parameters.

    2. Berendsen, H. J. C.; Grigera, J. R.; Straatsma, T. P. (1987). “The missing term in effective pair potentials.” J. Phys. Chem. 91, 6269. DOI: [10.1021/jp308312a]

      SPC/E water model. The original formulation of SPC/E.

    3. Mahoney, M. W.; Jorgensen, W. L. (2000). “Quantum, zero-point, and thermal heating of ice Ih to the melting point.” J. Chem. Phys. 112, 8910. DOI: [10.1063/1.481505]

      TIP4P water model. Four-site model with explicit charge sites.

    Books

    1. Frenkel, D.; Smit, B. (2002). Understanding Molecular Simulation: From Algorithms to Applications. 2nd ed. Academic Press.

      The standard textbook on MD. Chapters on Ewald, PPPM, and statistical mechanics of ensembles.

    2. Rapaport, D. C. (2004). The Art of Molecular Dynamics Simulation. 2nd ed. Cambridge University Press.

      Practical guide to MD implementation. Good for understanding integration algorithms.

    3. Allen, M. P.; Tildesley, D. J. (2017). Computer Simulation of Liquids. 2nd ed. Oxford University Press.

      Comprehensive reference on simulation methods. Ewald summation and PPPM are covered in detail.

    Data Analysis Tools

    1. MDAnalysis: https://www.mdanalysis.org

      Python library for analyzing MD trajectories. Supports LAMMPS formats.

    2. Ovito: https://www.ovito.org

      Visualization and analysis tool. Good for density profiles and particle visualization.

    3. pytim: https://pytim.readthedocs.io

      Python toolkit for interfacial MD analysis. Automatic liquid-vapor interface detection.

    Quick Reference: Key Equations

    Lennard-Jones Potential

    \[V(r) = 4\epsilon \left[ \left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^6 \right]\]

    Coulomb Potential

    \[V(r) = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r}\]

    Capacitance Matrix Relation

    \[\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{Q}_{0V} + \mathbf{C} \cdot \mathbf{V}\]

    Nosé-Hoover Thermostat (Extended Lagrangian)

    \[H' = H + \frac{Q}{2} \dot{s}^2 + (N+1) k_B T \ln s\]

    Virial Pressure

    \[P = \frac{Nk_BT}{V} + \frac{1}{3V}\left\langle \sum_i \mathbf{r}_i \cdot \mathbf{F}_i \right\rangle\]
