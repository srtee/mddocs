Constant Potential Molecular Dynamics in LAMMPS

A practical guide to running constant potential simulations with the ELECTRODE package

What Is This Guide?

This guide teaches you to run constant potential molecular dynamics (MD) simulations using the LAMMPS ELECTRODE package. It is written for researchers and students who have access to a working LAMMPS installation and want to understand the physics behind the commands — not just the syntax.

By “physics” we mean: - What does a Lennard-Jones potential actually describe? - Why does controlling temperature require a thermostat, and how do different thermostats work? - What does constant potential mean for an electrode, and why is it different from constant charge? - How does the capacitance matrix emerge from electrostatics?

What You Will Learn

By the end of this guide you will be able to:

Interpret LAMMPS commands in terms of physical meaning Every command linked to what it computes and why

Prerequisites

This guide assumes you have:

  • Introductory physics (mechanics, electromagnetism basics)
  • One semester of general chemistry (ions, Coulomb’s law)
  • Basic programming experience (any language)
  • Access to a LAMMPS installation with ELECTRODE and KSPACE packages enabled

No prior MD experience required. We explain the physics from scratch.

Computing Environment

All examples assume a standard LAMMPS installation. Commands like:

lmp -in in.electrode -pk gpu 1 -l yes

should be run in a terminal where LAMMPS is available.

To check if ELECTRODE is installed:

lmp -h | grep ELECTRODE
