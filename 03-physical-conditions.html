    What Is Being Held Constant?

    Real experiments control temperature, pressure, and composition. In MD, we choose which of these to hold fixed — this determines the statistical ensemble and thus which thermodynamic properties we can legitimately compute.

    Ensemble LAMMPS Fix What’s Fixed What Fluctuates Use Case
    NVE fix nve E, V, N T, P Microcanonical; equilibrium check
    NVT fix nvt or fix npt T, V, N E, P Canonical; most production
    NPT fix npt T, P, N E, V Isobaric; density-sensitive properties
    NPH fix nph P, H, N T, V Isobaric without thermostat (rare)

    The ensemble choice is not just convention — it determines which statistical mechanics formula you must use to connect simulation results to experimental observables.

    Time Integration and Timestep

    The Verlet Algorithm

    MD integrates Newton’s equations numerically. The velocity Verlet algorithm:

    # Velocity Verlet (per atom)
v(t + dt/2) = v(t) + 0.5 * F(t) / m * dt        # half-step velocity
x(t + dt) = x(t) + v(t + dt/2) * dt             # full-step position
F(t + dt) = forces(x(t + dt))                  # new forces
v(t + dt) = v(t + dt/2) + 0.5 * F(t + dt) / m * dt  # complete velocity

    Timestep Selection

    The timestep Δt must be small enough to resolve the fastest motion in the system:

    Motion Characteristic Frequency Δt Limit
    C-H bond stretch ~3000 cm⁻¹ ~0.5 fs
    O-H bond stretch ~3500 cm⁻¹ ~0.5 fs
    H-O-H bend ~1600 cm⁻¹ ~1 fs
    LJ repulsion Very fast requires small Δt
    Metal phonon ~5–10 ps period ~1–2 fs

    Typical values:

    • 1 fs for explicit water with flexible bonds
    • 0.5 fs for flexible molecules with H-bonds
    • 2 fs with bond constraints (fix shake or fix rattle)

    Why Timestep Matters for Output

    Δt Too Large Δt Too Small
    Energy drift; simulation “blows up” Wasted computation
    Atoms overlap catastrophically Results unchanged
    Timestep too large for physics Diminishing returns

    Validation: Run NVE and check energy conservation. Acceptable drift: < 0.1% of total energy over 1 ns.

    Bond Constraints

    fix shake or fix rattle removes fast degrees of freedom (C-H, O-H bonds) by constraint:

    fix constrain all shake 1e-4 200 0 a 1.0    # constrain bond with tolerance 1e-4

    Allows Δt = 2 fs (2× faster simulation, same accuracy for bonded motion).

    Thermostats — Controlling Temperature

    Temperature in MD

    Temperature is a statistical property, not a direct control variable:

    \[T = \frac{2}{3Nk_B} \left\langle \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \right\rangle\]

    The instantaneous temperature fluctuates. Thermostats manipulate atom velocities to sample the canonical ensemble correctly.

    Thermostat Algorithms

    Velocity Rescaling (fix temp/rescale)

    fix tempctr all temp/rescale 10 300.0 300.0 0.02 1.0

    Simple scaling: multiply all velocities to match target T. Not rigorous — does not produce correct fluctuations. Useful for quick equilibration.

    Berendsen Thermostat (fix berendsen)

    fix ber all berendsen temp 300.0 300.0 100.0

    Weak coupling to heat bath. Produces incorrect fluctuations (variance too low). Not recommended for production.

    Nosé-Hoover Thermostat (fix nvt)

    fix nvt_all all nvt temp 300.0 300.0 100.0

    Extended system method. The system + thermostat share energy. Produces correct canonical distribution. Standard for most production runs.

    Variants: - fix nvt/kk — Kokkos version for GPU - fix nvt/sphere — for angular degrees of freedom

    Langevin Thermostat (fix langevin)

    fix mylang all langevin 300.0 300.0 100.0 12345

    Stochastic differential equation:

    \[m\frac{dv}{dt} = F - \gamma mv + \sqrt{2\gamma m k_B T} \, \xi(t)\]

    Term Physical Meaning
    F Conservative force
    γmv Damping (friction)
    √(2γmk_BT) ξ(t) Random noise (thermal fluctuations)

    Good for: - Non-equilibrium dynamics (shear, field) - Systems with heterogeneous dynamics - When you want explicit noise

    Parameter choice: - 100.0 = damping time (fd). Too small = over-damped; too large = slow equipartition - 12345 = random seed (set explicitly for reproducibility)

    Thermostat Comparison

    Thermostat Fluctuation Correct? ergodicity Computational Cost Best Use
    temp/rescale ❌ No N/A Low Quick equilibration
    berendsen ❌ No Poor Low Not recommended
    nvt (Nosé-Hoover) ✅ Yes Good Medium Standard production
    langevin ✅ Yes Good Medium Non-equilibrium
    nvt + chain ✅ Yes Best High Difficult systems

    What Temperature Controls Physically

    Temperature Change Physical Effect Observable
    Increase T Higher kinetic energy → faster diffusion MSD, diffusion coefficient
    Too high T Bond breaking, chemical reactions (LAMMPS not suited) Decomposition
    Too low T Slow dynamics, metastable states Long equilibration times
    T fluctuations too small Wrong ensemble (Berendsen) Incorrect heat capacity

    Barostats — Controlling Pressure

    Pressure in MD

    Pressure from virial theorem:

    \[P = \frac{Nk_BT}{V} + \frac{1}{3V}\left\langle \sum_i \mathbf{r}_i \cdot \mathbf{F}_i \right\rangle\]

    Term Physical Meaning
    Nk_B T / V Kinetic contribution (ideal gas)
    ⟨Σ r·F⟩ / 3V Virial (force contribution)

    The mechanical pressure is computed from atomic forces and positions. For anisotropic systems (slabs), the pressure tensor has different components.

    Barostat Algorithms

    Nose-Hoover Barostat (fix npt)

    fix npt_all all npt temp 300.0 300.0 100.0 iso 1.0 1.0 500.0

    Controls T (Nosé-Hoover) and P (Nosé-Hoover). The iso keyword applies isotropic pressure control.

    Keywords: - iso — isotropic (all components same P) - aniso — anisotropic (x, y, z independently) - semiiso — semi-isotropic (xy coupled, z separate)

    For slab geometries with non-periodic z:

    fix npt_xz all npt temp 300.0 300.0 100.0 semiiso 1.0 1.0 500.0

    This controls P_x = P_y, but allows P_z to adjust independently (or vice versa depending on setup).

    MTK Barostat

    Martyna-Tobias-Klein barostat (more reliable for non-equilibrium):

    fix npt_mtk all npt temp 300.0 300.0 100.0 aniso 1.0 1.0 500.0

    Use for: - Anisotropic systems - Systems with external fields - When pressure tensor components matter

    What Pressure Controls Physically

    Pressure Change Physical Effect Observable
    Increase P Compress system; atoms closer Lower volume; higher density
    NPT equilibration System reaches correct density Density ~1 g/cm³ for water
    Anisotropic P Different stress in different directions Lattice parameters
    Too high P Over-compression; unrealistic Density too high

    Integrators in LAMMPS

    Fix Ensemble Velocity Update Position Update
    fix nve NVE Verlet (no thermostat) Verlet
    fix nvt NVT Nosé-Hoover Verlet
    fix npt NPT Nosé-Hoover (T) + barostat Box changes
    fix nph NPH Barostat only Box changes

    Combinations with ELECTRODE

    For constant potential simulations:

    fix frozen all nve                      # frozen electrodes (default)
fix mylang elyte langevin 300 300 100 12345  # electrolyte thermostat

# OR for frozen electrodes with NVT on electrolyte:
fix mynvt elyte nvt temp 300.0 300.0 100.0
    WarningDon’t Double-Thermostat

    If you apply two thermostats to the same atoms, they will fight each other. Use EITHER fix nvt OR fix langevin on a given group — not both.

    Observables — What You Actually Measure

    Thermodynamic Output

    thermo 100
thermo_style custom step temp pe ke vol density
    Variable Physical Meaning Normal Behavior
    Temp Instantaneous temperature Fluctuates ±10 K around target
    PotEng Potential energy Stable; small drift acceptable
    KinEng Kinetic energy Fluctuates with T
    TotEng Total energy Conserved in NVE; drifts in NVT/NPT
    Vol System volume Constant in NVT; adjusts in NPT
    Density Mass/volume Should match experimental density
    Press Total pressure Fluctuates; mean matches external P

    Computes for Analysis

    compute temp_elec elyte temp     # electrolyte temperature (exclude frozen)
compute stress all stress/atom  # per-atom stress tensor
compute RDF all rdf 2 * 500     # radial distribution function

    Fix-Specific Output (ELECTRODE)

    For fix electrode/conp:

    variable Q_bot equal f_fxconp[5]    # charge on bottom electrode (check index)
variable Q_top equal f_fxconp[6]    # charge on top electrode
variable V_bot equal f_fxconp[2]    # potential on bottom (for conq)

    The exact indices depend on the fix configuration. Check LAMMPS documentation or print all fix values.

    Common Mistakes

    Mistake Consequence Correction
    Thermostatting frozen atoms No dynamics (atoms can’t move) Only thermostat mobile atoms
    Using Berendsen for production Wrong heat capacity Use Nosé-Hoover or Langevin
    Timestep too large Energy drift, crash Reduce Δt or constrain bonds
    Forgetting barostat for density Wrong equilibrium density Use NPT to equilibrate density
    Anisotropic pressure for isotropic system Stressed state, wrong density Use iso pressure control
